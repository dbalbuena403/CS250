# CS250

How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating “user stories” help with this? 

 

 

I analyze user requirements to determine the objectives that users wish to achieve, to identify the problems that users are attempting to resolve, and to establish which features will provide the greatest benefit. For the SNHU Travel project, the objective was to develop software that enables users to investigate well-known travel locations. Through examining the project's requirements and acceptance criteria, I gained an understanding of what the software should be able to do and implemented the necessary functionality. 

       User stories are used to convert user requirements into simple, actionable specifications. Typically, user stories follow the syntax "as a, I would like too so that I can." This syntax allows developers to keep their focus on what users are trying to accomplish rather than how they will go about doing it. Additionally, user stories allow developers to prioritize, set boundaries for completion of features, and know when a feature is done. Ultimately, using user stories will allow you to produce software that meets the needs of your users. 

   How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work? 

 

     When I start a new software project, I break it down into smaller parts. First, I understand what needs to be done. Then, I identify the primary features of the project and plan out how I am going to build those features. Next, I create the code and run tests to ensure that everything is working properly. Finally, I review the output and modify the code if needed. Through my work on the SNHU Travel project, I have learned the importance of using a well-defined development process as a guide for building software while maintaining the flexibility to adjust my plans as needed based upon changing requirements or priorities. 

      In my future development projects, I plan to utilize agile practices including sprint planning, daily scrum meetings, sprint reviews, and sprint retrospectives. Sprint planning will allow us to define attainable objectives for each project, communication through daily scrum meetings will enable us to recognize issues before they become major problems; sprint reviews will give us the ability to showcase completed works and receive input from our stakeholders, and retrospectives will provide a platform for our team to reflect upon successful aspects of the current project as well as areas of improvement for the upcoming project. Additionally, I intend to use the product backlog to prioritize and manage tasks to ensure that our team provides the highest quality service to our customers. 

        What does it mean to be a good team member in software development? 

 

 

   To be an effective team player in software development involves communicating effectively with other team members; respecting their diverse viewpoints; completing your assigned tasks; and working together to reach the collective objective of the team. As a team player, you are willing to ask questions of your fellow team members; assist them as needed; receive feedback from your peers as a learning experience; and take ownership of issues that develop during the process of developing software. Software development is a collaborative effort, which means that individual team members may provide varying levels of expertise and viewpoints on a particular project. 

          In an agile environment, a great team member takes part in scrum events; informs other members of the team on what has been accomplished; and identifies possible issues that could grow into larger ones. Team members must always be ready for change and continuously improve themselves, instead of pointing fingers if things do not turn out exactly how you wanted them to. The SNHU travel project showed me that I needed to understand the requirements, use acceptance criteria, and review my own work to make sure it met the standards expected of me. I think that being accountable for your actions, adapting quickly, and communicating clearly with others will help you contribute to the success of a software development team.
